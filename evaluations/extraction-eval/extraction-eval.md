# Extraction evaluation — pipeline output vs golden reference set

**Method.** `evaluations/extraction-eval/golden_rules.json` is a hand-curated reference ruleset produced
independently (Claude Opus, human-in-the-loop, full-document review) — it was NOT generated by
this repo's pipeline. We score the pipeline's `data/rules.json` against it: every golden rule
should be represented (recall); pipeline rules outside golden's scope are acceptable unless
hallucinated (precision). Golden's own scope note declares consumer support, vulnerable-consumer
chapters, 1:1 interactions and distribution-chain duties deliberately out of its scope.

**Recall: 12/13 (92%).** Mapping:

|---|---|---|
| CP-01 good outcomes | CP-01 | ✓ |

| CU-01 clear/fair/not misleading | CU-04 | ✓ |

| CU-07 in good time | CU-03 (timeliness limb) | ✓ |

**Root cause of the miss (verified, not assumed):** the behavioural-exploitation obligations
live in the legal instrument at PRIN 2A.2.3G(b)–(c) / 2A.2.10G(2), PDF pp.108–109 — pages the
subset did not include (only PRIN 2A.5, pp.127–131, was operator-added). Ch 5 prose mentions
biases only in passing. The extractor cannot extract text it never saw: a **subset coverage
gap, not an extraction-quality failure**. The miss mattered: CC-04 is the rule that catches the
brief's canonical "get rich tomorrow 🚀🚀🚀" case on urgency/excitement grounds.

**Precision:** no hallucinated rules — all 31 trace to real document text. Three rules
(CU-15/16 one-to-one interactions, CU-17 distribution chain) fall in golden's declared
process-flavoured exclusions; CS-*/VC-* rules are outside golden's scope but valid extractions
from chapters the planner scoped in.

## Fix

Extend the operator-added made-rules range to include Principle 12 + PRIN 2A.1–2A.2:

```
npm run cut -- \
  --extra "Appendix 1 / Principle 12 and PRIN 2A.1–2A.2 cross-cutting obligations — made rules:104-110" \
  --extra "Appendix 1 / PRIN 2A.5 Consumer understanding — made rules:127-131"
npm run extract
```

## Round 2 — after fix (subset ~14.5k words)

**Recall: 13/13 (100%).** The previously missed obligation now extracts as two standalone
high/medium rules: CC-02 ("must not manipulate or exploit customers' emotions, behavioural
biases or vulnerabilities to create demand or mislead") and CC-04 (exploiting inertia /
over-optimism causing foreseeable harm). The wider made-rules context also produced CC-05
("must not imply the risks do not exist") and CU-13 ("disclosure as a shield") — both strong,
text-checkable additions.

Notes from round 2:
- Re-extraction is non-deterministic: the rule inventory reshuffled (31 → 31 rules but
  different splits; e.g. golden CC-03 "enable objectives" is now covered via CU-01/CU-03
  rather than a standalone cross-cutting rule). Coverage of golden is complete; granularity
  differs run to run.
- Three process-leaning rules appeared (CU-11 testing, CU-12 adapt-on-evidence, CC-07 redress)
  that golden scopes out. Kept: they mostly return `not_applicable` for a static marketing
  text and cost little; a stricter extractor instruction is a possible refinement.

## Findings about the golden set itself

Golden is well-constructed as a baseline (declared scope, binding-source citations, checkable
phrasing), with three findings:

1. **Gap:** golden CU-08 lists the audience/complexity/channel limbs of PRIN 2A.5.8R but omits
   the fourth limb — tailoring to **the firm's role** (advice vs information-only boundary).
   Text-checkable and highly relevant for a trading platform ("we tell you exactly what to
   buy" from an execution-only broker). The pipeline extracted it in both rounds (round 1
   CU-14, round 2 CC-08).
2. Golden CC-02's `check` has inverted polarity ("Could this… lead to harm?" — YES = breach),
   the same slip our round-1 extraction made in its CC-02. Two different models, identical
   polarity error on the foreseeable-harm rule.
3. Minor citation drift: golden places PRIN 2A.5.3R at "PDF p.126"; the 2A.5 text begins at
   p.127 (verified by content probe).

## Round 3 — fully autonomous page selection (scan pipeline, no hardcoded ranges)

Round 2's fix was a human hardcoding page ranges — rejected as a design (recall must not
depend on an operator knowing the document). Replaced the TOC-only planner with the `scan`
stage: every page of the PDF is classified by the LLM in parallel windows (DECISIONS.md
D11/D12). Two iterations were needed:

1. **Scan selectivity.** First scan marked 139/161 pages relevant — a criteria contradiction
   (blanket "legal-instrument rule text is relevant" bullet) plus "mentions communications"
   false positives. Fix: instrument text passes the same checkable-from-text test as prose;
   mentioning communications inside a process obligation doesn't qualify. Second scan: 78/161,
   with every golden-critical range selected autonomously — including PRIN 2A.2 pp.104–113,
   the range a human had to hardcode in round 2.
2. **Parent-obligation absorption.** First extract over the (noisier, 31k-word) autonomous
   subset scored 12/13: "avoid foreseeable harm" disappeared as a standalone rule — its
   guidance examples (exploiting biases, vulnerability) were extracted while the parent
   obligation was absorbed, an extractor miss this time (the source pages WERE in the subset).
   Fix: a document-agnostic COVERAGE FLOOR instruction — where the regulation enumerates
   named overarching obligations, each must be emitted as its own rule; guidance never absorbs
   its parent. Re-extract: **13/13**, with all three cross-cutting parents standalone.

Final state: recall 13/13 against golden with zero human page selection. The two fixes
generalize: (a) scan criteria must apply one uniform checkability test to all text forms;
(b) extraction needs an explicit floor tied to the document's own enumerated structure.

## Round 4 — re-run after cleaning `data/` (autonomous scan → cut → extract)

Regenerated from scratch (empty `data/`, no hardcoded ranges). Scan: **73/161 pages, 28 ranges**
selected autonomously (incl. the PRIN 2A instrument pp.104–112, 127–131). Extract: **31 rules**
across 7 self-derived categories (`consumer_principle`, `cross_cutting`, `consumer_understanding`,
`consumer_support`, `price_and_value`, `scope_and_territorial`, `redress_and_harm_communications`;
prefixes CP/CC/CU/CS/PV/SC/RD). Note: a *third distinct taxonomy* from earlier runs (38→33→31
rules, prefixes reshuffled each time) — confirms the extractor's non-determinism (D13/D16). Recall
is unaffected because we score by MEANING, not ID.

**One thing to watch — the benefit/risk-balance obligation is not a standalone rule.** Golden's
CU-03 ("promote benefits ⇒ show risks with prominence") is woven into three rules' `check` fields
instead: CC-03 ("claims framed without downplaying associated risks"), CU-02 ("balanced… capable
of substantiation") and CU-07 ("significant risks… sufficient prominence"). Functionally covered
— the checker still catches unbalanced ads (checker-eval case 04) — but a reviewer scanning
`rules.json` for a dedicated risk-warning rule won't find one. If risk-balance is a priority, the
coverage-floor instruction could name it explicitly.

**Precision / scope — broader than golden (31 vs 13), by design not by error.** Golden was
deliberately scoped to marketing-text-checkable rules only; this run additionally extracts
price & value (PV-01…03), professional-only scope (SC-01/02), redress (RD-01) and consumer
support (CS-01…05) — all legitimate document obligations, several genuinely checkable against a
promotion (intro-discount transparency PV-02, free/"data-cost" PV-03, professional-only disclosure
SC-01). Mild scope creep: CU-10 (one-to-one), CU-11 (testing), CU-12/CU-13 (distribution-chain)
are firm-process rules not verifiable from a static text — harmless (the checker returns
`not_applicable`) but trimmable if a tighter marketing-only ruleset is wanted. No hallucinations.

## Takeaway

The extractor itself is strong (92% recall on first sight of the text, 100% after the subset
gap was closed; zero hallucinations; the pipeline caught a real gap in the human-curated golden
set). The two dominant quality levers, in order: **input coverage** (which pages the LLM sees —
solved structurally by the scan stage's every-page guarantee, not by prompt wording) and
**enumeration mirroring** (the coverage-floor instruction preventing guidance from absorbing
parent obligations). Verdict-level prompt tuning mattered less than either.
